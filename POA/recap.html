Accounting Basics

Accounting is the process of recording, classifying, summarizing, and interpreting financial information.

Assets

Resources owned by a business.

Examples: Cash, Inventory, Equipment.

Liabilities

Amounts owed by the business.

Examples: Loans, Creditors.

Capital

Owner's investment in the business.

Revenue

Income earned from business activities.

Accounting Equation

Assets = Capital + Liabilities

If assets increase by buying equipment with a loan, liabilities also increase.

Double Entry Rules

Account Increase Decrease
Assets Debit Credit
Liabilities Credit Debit
Capital Credit Debit
Expenses Debit Credit
Revenue Credit Debit

Example

Purchased furniture for cash $2,000.

  • Furniture Account → Debit $2,000
  • Cash Account → Credit $2,000

Books of Original Entry

Sales Journal

Records credit sales of goods.

Purchases Journal

Records credit purchases.

Cash Book

Records all cash and bank transactions.

General Journal

Used for corrections and special entries.

Ledger Accounts

Ledgers contain all accounts of the business.

Main Types

  • General Ledger
  • Sales Ledger
  • Purchases Ledger

T-Account Layout

Debit side is left. Credit side is right.

Balance carried down = difference between both sides.

Trial Balance

A list of all ledger balances used to check arithmetic accuracy.

  • Total debits must equal total credits.
  • Does not guarantee all records are correct.

Errors NOT Found by Trial Balance

  • Error of omission
  • Error of principle
  • Compensating errors
  • Error of commission

Bank Reconciliation

Used to reconcile differences between the cash book and bank statement.

Add:

  • Outstanding deposits

Subtract:

  • Unpresented cheques

Updated Cash Book Balance ± Reconciling Items = Bank Statement Balance

Depreciation

Reduction in value of non-current assets over time.

Straight Line

(Cost - Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life

Reducing Balance

Percentage applied to book value yearly.

Asset cost = $10,000

Residual value = $2,000

Useful life = 4 years

Depreciation = $2,000 yearly

Adjustments

Accrued Expense

Expense owing but not yet paid.

Prepaid Expense

Expense paid in advance.

Accrued Income

Income earned but not yet received.

Income Received in Advance

Money received before earning it.

Final Accounts

Trading Account

Used to calculate gross profit.

Sales - Cost of Sales = Gross Profit

Income Statement

Gross Profit + Other Income - Expenses = Net Profit

Statement of Financial Position

Shows assets, liabilities, and capital.

Control Accounts

Used to check accuracy of debtors and creditors ledgers.

Sales Ledger Control

Tracks debtors.

Purchases Ledger Control

Tracks creditors.

Suspense Accounts

Temporary account used when trial balance disagrees.

Correction Example

If purchases account understated by $500:

  • Debit Purchases $500
  • Credit Suspense $500

Ratios

Current Ratio

Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

Acid Test Ratio

(Current Assets - Inventory) ÷ Current Liabilities

Gross Profit %

Gross Profit ÷ Sales × 100

Net Profit %

Net Profit ÷ Sales × 100

Rate of Stock Turnover

Cost of Sales ÷ Average Inventory

Return on Capital Employed

Net Profit ÷ Capital Employed × 100

Manufacturing Accounts

  • Prime Cost = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses
  • Factory Overheads are added to production costs.
  • Cost of Production transfers to Trading Account.

Incomplete Records

Used when full double entry records are missing.

Important Formulas

  • Opening Capital + Profit + Additional Capital - Drawings = Closing Capital
  • Mark-up = Profit based on Cost
  • Margin = Profit based on Selling Price

Correction of Errors

Error Type Meaning
Omission Transaction completely missing
Commission Correct amount but wrong account
Principle Wrong class of account used
Original Entry Wrong amount entered everywhere

Exam Tips

  • Always label accounts properly.
  • Show workings clearly.
  • Balance accounts neatly.
  • Read adjustments twice.
  • Underline totals.
  • Check whether values should be added or subtracted.
  • Watch out for drawings and additional capital.
  • Do not panic if trial balance disagrees.
  • Memorize ratio formulas.
  • Attempt every question.

Quick Fire Recap

1. Which side increases assets?

Debit

2. What account records credit sales?

Sales Journal

3. What is depreciation?

Reduction in value of non-current assets over time.

4. What is the formula for current ratio?

Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

5. What does a suspense account do?

Temporarily holds differences in the trial balance.

Last Minute Memory Triggers

  • Debit the receiver, credit the giver.
  • Expenses and assets usually debit.
  • Income and liabilities usually credit.
  • Gross Profit comes before expenses.
  • Net Profit comes after expenses.
  • Current assets are short-term assets.
  • Current liabilities are short-term debts.
  • Adjustments affect two accounts.
  • Closing inventory goes to Trading Account and SOFP.
